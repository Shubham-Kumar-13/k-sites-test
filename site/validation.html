Validation of the measured knockout evidence

K-Sites rates a gene from what has been measured when the gene is lost. This page checks how far those measurements can be trusted, how well the estimate for genes without a measurement works, and how informative the pleiotropy score is. The numbers below are produced by tools/evaluate_knockout_evidence.py; they describe the data version named below and are refreshed with it.

5. How good is the estimate for genes without a knockout result?

Most genes were never knocked out in a mouse. For them K-Sites estimates the chance that the knockout is lethal from the measurements of the human ortholog (share of cancer cell lines that need the gene, how rarely people carry loss-of-function variants, tissue expression, close paralogs). The model was fitted on 12,234 mouse genes with a known outcome (3,502 lethal, 8,732 viable) and tested on genes it had not seen (5-fold cross-validation).

  • Area under the ROC curve: 0.835 (0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect).
  • Flagging the genes estimated at 0.3 or higher finds 65.1% of the lethal genes and wrongly flags 13.6% of the viable ones. For comparison, the keyword prediction in section 6 found 23% and wrongly flagged 13%.

A large separation means the organism's lethal calls tell essential genes from non-essential ones. Mouse (a systematic screen plus the literature) separates them best. For fly and worm the separation is small because their databases record lethal mutants far more often than viable ones: a gene is in the database mostly because a mutant was found that died. For them a lethal call is a hint to confirm in the literature, not a measurement of how many genes are essential, and the share of "non-essential" genes called lethal is inflated by that selection, not necessarily by errors. The site says so before a fly, worm or zebrafish run.

What these checks do not show

  • They do not prove that a knockout will work in your hands. A measurement says what happened in the published mice, yeast or cell lines; strain, allele, cell type, culture conditions and the way the gene is removed all matter.
  • The two mouse sources are not fully independent. IMPC alleles are often knockout-first or deletion alleles that can be hypomorphic, while literature nulls are often classical alleles; where they disagree the report shows both and rates the gene at least HIGH_CONCERN if any line was lethal.
  • Absence of a lethality annotation is weak evidence of viability. K-Sites therefore counts a knockout as viable only when the null animals were examined as young or adult animals (or declared normal); genes with only embryonic findings stay "not verified".
  • Cell-line essentiality comes from cancer cell lines (DepMap 24Q4). Primary cells, stem cells and other cell types can differ.
  • Zebrafish, fly and worm are measured only for the genes their community databases cover. ZFIN, FlyBase and WormBase curate mutants that were studied for a reason, so the covered genes are biased towards interesting phenotypes (and, for zebrafish, towards recent CRISPR mutants); fish with morpholinos, transgenes or heterozygous genotypes are left out. WormBase does not say which alleles are null, so a gene is called viable only when two or more alleles are reported not lethal, and a lethal call that rests on RNAi alone is marked as such. Genes without a measurement of their own, Xenopus, and every plant or other organism have no measured data; for zebrafish, fly, worm and Xenopus the knockout of the mouse ortholog is shown as a hint, never as a measurement. Arabidopsis has no openly licensed machine-readable knockout resource that could be used.

How to reproduce

python tools/build_evidence_data.py --out .cache/evidence-out        # builds the data files (downloads the sources once)
python tools/evaluate_knockout_evidence.py --out VALIDATION.md       # re-runs sections 1-5 and 7
python tools/evaluate_knockout_evidence.py --predictor 60            # adds section 6 (needs the network, about 20 minutes)
python -m pytest tests/test_paper_truth.py                            # the published-knockout check as a unit test

The published-knockout list is tests/fixtures/benchmark/paper_truth.json; every entry cites the paper whose abstract was read.
